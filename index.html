Case Study 01

Opsly HR ↗

Workforce management platform connecting businesses with operational talent at scale

Built End-to-End Workforce Tech Marketplace SaaS
Live Product ↗ GitHub

Overview

Opsly HR is a workforce management platform I built end-to-end. It connects businesses with operational talent across multiple roles — virtual assistants, customer support agents, and product operations specialists — while managing the full employment lifecycle on both sides.

Problem

Hiring operational talent is fragmented and unreliable. Businesses struggle to find and retain skilled support, while talented professionals lack structured visibility, fair access to opportunities, and consistent income. Existing platforms don't solve the end-to-end management problem.

My Role & Approach

I owned this product end-to-end — from market research and system architecture through to design, engineering collaboration, and launch. I conducted user discovery with both businesses and independent talent to validate the core problem before writing a single line of product spec. Rather than building a simple listings board, I designed a multi-sided platform with three distinct portals, each purpose-built for different engagement types: direct hire, managed engagements, and project-based contracts.

PM Work

I ran the full product management process — from user research and problem framing through to roadmapping, sprint planning, and stakeholder alignment. I defined the product vision, wrote detailed PRDs for each portal, prioritised features using impact-effort scoring, and managed the engineering backlog across multiple build cycles. I also led go-to-market strategy and early user onboarding.

System Architecture

The platform was structured around three interconnected portals — talent, client, and admin — each with purpose-built workflows including vetting pipelines, automated contract generation, time tracking, milestone-based payments, and performance dashboards.

Vetting Pipeline → Contract Engine → Time Tracking → Payments → Admin Portal

Execution

I led system design, built product specs and wireframes, worked directly with engineering to implement scalable infrastructure, and iterated through multiple feedback cycles with early users. Every workflow — from onboarding to payment — was designed and refined by me. The codebase is open-source and publicly available on GitHub.

Outcome

Opsly HR is live at opslyhr.com — a fully operational platform that not only connects talent to opportunities, but manages the complete operational complexity behind those engagements. The result is a workforce management system, not just a job board.

Case Study 03

Skeduley ↗

Intelligent scheduling SaaS built for teams and service businesses

Built End-to-End Scheduling SaaS AI-Powered
Live Product ↗ GitHub

Overview

Skeduley is an intelligent scheduling SaaS I built end-to-end, designed to replace the friction of manual scheduling for service businesses, consultants, and teams. The product handles availability management, automated booking, and smart conflict resolution in one cohesive system.

Problem

Scheduling remains one of the most operationally costly activities for service businesses — consuming time on both sides, producing unnecessary back-and-forth, and creating breakdowns when things change. Existing tools are either too simple or too heavyweight for small to mid-size teams.

My Role & Approach

I designed and built Skeduley from the ground up — architecting the scheduling logic, designing the user experience, leading engineering, and launching the product. My approach centred on eliminating friction at every touchpoint: clients self-serve, providers stay in control, and the system handles the coordination automatically.

PM Work

I conducted competitive analysis across scheduling tools to identify underserved segments and sharpen the positioning. I ran discovery interviews with service business owners, translated insights into a structured product roadmap, and prioritised the MVP feature set based on activation and retention signals. I wrote all product specifications, managed the build cycle, and iterated based on beta user feedback before the public launch.

System Design

The platform includes availability configuration, smart booking flows, automated reminders, calendar integrations, buffer management, group scheduling, and a provider dashboard with analytics. The AI layer surfaces intelligent suggestions for availability optimisation and detects scheduling conflicts before they happen.

Execution

I drove every layer of this product — from the backend scheduling logic and data model to the front-end experience and onboarding flow. I ran user research, iterated based on feedback, and managed the roadmap through to live release.

Outcome

Skeduley is live at skeduley.com — a complete scheduling system that removes the coordination overhead from service businesses and gives providers and clients a seamless, reliable booking experience.

Philosophy

How I Think About Products

Build to understand, not just to specify

I've found that the fastest path to a great product is being close enough to the build to see what's actually happening — not just handing off specs and waiting.

Systems over features

Every feature exists inside a system. I focus on how things work together, not just what gets built. The right architecture makes future work easier — the wrong one makes it exponentially harder.

Clarity before scale

A product must make sense before it grows. Scaling confusion only produces bigger problems faster. I insist on getting the core model right before pushing for growth.

Operations is product

The best user experience is often invisible — it's the system behind the product that keeps everything running reliably. I design operations as carefully as I design features.

Business and user alignment

The best products solve real user problems while supporting clear business outcomes. I treat this alignment as a design constraint, not an afterthought.

I'm most effective where product, engineering, and operations overlap — and where the work actually ships.
